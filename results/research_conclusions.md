# Key Findings

#### The principal empirical conclusions from the cryptocurrency liquidity study spanning 76 major negative events between 2020 and 2024.

- Liquidity shocks on BitCoin are large: abnormal bid-ask spreads peak at an average of 6.45 baseline standard deviations compared to pre-event level.
- Recovery is rapid but variable: the median time to return below 1.5 × baseline spread is 60 minutes (mean 113 minutes), based on a 20-minute moving-average criterion.
- Shock magnitude and recovery duration are strongly linked: the correlation between standardized peak spread and recovery length is 0.51 (Pearson) and 0.70 (Spearman), indicating longer disruptions after larger dislocations.
- Event typology has limited explanatory power: macroeconomic, regulatory, and crypto-native events exhibit no statistically significant differences in either shock intensity or recovery time under the panel framework.
- Data-processing safeguards—merge tolerances, baseline continuity checks, and coverage flags—ensure that the minute-level panels remain reliable even when source feeds differ in depth and completeness.

These conclusions rely on panels generated from high-frequency trade and quote data aggregated to the minute level, with baseline windows of 30 days and post-event observation windows of 10 days.
